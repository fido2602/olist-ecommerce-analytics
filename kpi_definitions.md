KPI Definitions — Olist E-Commerce Analytics



1\. Purpose



This document defines the key performance indicators (KPIs) used to evaluate sales performance, order activity, delivery reliability, customer satisfaction, and repeat purchasing in the Olist E-Commerce Analytics project.



The definitions establish a consistent basis for interpreting dashboard metrics, validating Power BI calculations against SQL analysis, and communicating business findings.



Important: The definitions below describe the intended business logic. Final validation requires confirming that the Power BI measures, filters, and underlying SQL queries use the same calculation rules and populations.

3\. KPI Definitions and Validation



3.1 Total Sales



Purpose: Measure the sales value generated by qualifying order items.



Definition: Sum of item prices for the orders included in the selected analysis population.



Calculation logic: Sum of qualifying order-item prices.



Freight treatment: Excludes freight charges unless the report explicitly defines sales to include freight.



Validation checks:



Reconcile the Power BI result against the corresponding SQL analysis.



Confirm the order-status and date filters.



Ensure item sales and payment totals are not treated as interchangeable measures.



Document any differences caused by population or metric definitions.



Business interpretation: Total sales measures the value of qualifying items sold. It should not automatically be interpreted as profit, net revenue, or total customer payment.



3.2 Total Orders



Purpose: Measure the volume of qualifying orders.



Definition: Count of distinct order IDs included in the selected analysis population.



Calculation logic: Count distinct order\_id values after applying the intended filters.



Validation checks:



Confirm which order statuses are included.



Ensure joins to order-item, payment, or review tables do not inflate the count.



Reconcile the Power BI measure with the corresponding SQL result.



Business interpretation: Order volume provides context for sales, average order value, and operational performance.



3.3 Average Order Value (AOV)



Purpose: Measure the average sales value associated with each qualifying order.



Definition: Qualifying sales amount divided by the number of qualifying orders.



Calculation logic: Total qualifying sales ÷ distinct qualifying orders.



Validation checks:



Use consistent filters and populations for the numerator and denominator.



Confirm whether the sales amount includes item prices only or also includes freight.



Ensure orders with multiple items are counted once in the denominator.



Compare the result with SQL using the same definition.



Business interpretation: AOV indicates the average item-sales value per qualifying order. It does not measure profit per order.



3.4 On-Time Delivery Rate



Purpose: Evaluate delivery reliability against the promised delivery date.



Definition: Percentage of eligible delivered orders delivered on or before the estimated delivery date.



Calculation logic: Eligible delivered orders delivered on or before the promised date ÷ all eligible delivered orders × 100.



Validation checks:



Confirm the actual date comparison used in Power BI.



Distinguish timestamp-based comparisons from calendar-day comparisons.



Exclude orders that do not meet the defined delivery eligibility criteria.



Confirm how missing delivery or estimated-delivery timestamps are handled.



Reconcile the result with SQL using the same definition.



Business interpretation: A higher rate indicates that a larger share of eligible delivered orders met or beat the promised delivery date.



Definition note: Timestamp-based and calendar-day approaches can produce different counts. The final published KPI must match the actual Power BI measure.



3.5 Average Review Score



Purpose: Measure customer satisfaction using submitted review scores.



Definition: Average of the available review scores in the selected analysis population.



Calculation logic: Sum of qualifying review scores ÷ count of qualifying review scores.



Validation checks:



Confirm how missing scores are handled.



Check for duplicate review records and determine the appropriate review population.



Confirm the effects of report filters and relationships between reviews and orders.



Reconcile the measure with SQL using the same population.

3.6 Unique Customers



Purpose: Measure the number of distinct customers represented in the qualifying order population.



Definition: Count of distinct customer\_unique\_id values associated with the selected analysis population.



Calculation logic: Count distinct customer\_unique\_id values after applying the intended filters.



Validation checks:



Confirm that customer\_unique\_id, rather than customer\_id, is used when measuring distinct customers.



Apply consistent order-status, date, and other report filters.



Ensure table joins do not inflate the distinct-customer count.



Reconcile the result with the SQL customer analysis.



SQL analysis finding: The customer analysis identified 96,096 unique customers. Verify that the Power BI measure uses the same population before publishing this as the final dashboard value.



Business interpretation: Unique customers measure the distinct customer population, not the number of orders or customer records.



3.7 Repeat Customers



Purpose: Measure how many distinct customers placed more than one qualifying order.



Definition: Count of distinct customer\_unique\_id values associated with more than one qualifying order in the defined analysis population.



Calculation logic: Identify customers with more than one qualifying distinct order, then count those customers.



Validation checks:



Use customer\_unique\_id to identify the same customer across multiple orders.



Confirm the order population and qualifying statuses.



Check how same-day orders are treated.



Ensure the result matches the SQL customer analysis.



SQL analysis finding: 2,997 repeat customers were identified among 96,096 unique customers.



Business interpretation: Repeat customers provide a measure of observed repeat purchasing. Multiple orders do not necessarily establish long-term loyalty or retention.



3.8 Repeat Customer Rate



Purpose: Measure the proportion of unique customers who placed more than one qualifying order.



Definition: Repeat customers divided by all eligible unique customers in the same analysis population, multiplied by 100.



Calculation logic: Repeat customers ÷ eligible unique customers × 100.



SQL analysis finding: Using 2,997 repeat customers and 96,096 unique customers, the repeat customer rate is approximately 3.12%.



Validation checks:



Ensure the numerator and denominator use the same order population and filters.



Confirm the customer identifier and repeat-purchase rule.



Document whether same-day orders count as repeat purchases.



Keep the overall repeat customer rate separate from cohort-based repeat rates.



Business interpretation: This rate measures the share of the observed customer population with multiple qualifying orders. It is not equivalent to a time-based retention rate.



4\. Cross-KPI Validation Standards



Before publishing final KPI values, verify the following:



Consistent populations: Numerators and denominators use compatible filters and eligibility rules.



Correct granularity: Orders, order items, customers, and reviews are counted at their intended levels.



Join integrity: One-to-many joins do not unintentionally duplicate sales, orders, or review scores.



Date logic: Delivery calculations use a documented comparison rule.



Metric reconciliation: Power BI results are compared with SQL outputs using equivalent definitions.



Transparent limitations: Unresolved discrepancies and assumptions are documented rather than hidden.



5\. Interpretation and Reporting Principles



Sales value should not be presented as profit.



Item sales and total payments should not be assumed to reconcile without checking their definitions and populations.



Order counts should use distinct order IDs where appropriate.



Average review scores should be interpreted alongside review coverage and data quality.



Repeat purchasing is not automatically proof of long-term retention.



Correlations between delivery performance and review scores should not be described as proof of causation.



Reported KPI values should be labeled as validated only after their Power BI calculations have been checked against the corresponding SQL analysis.
